The 60-second answer · planning judgment

Shortlist Brussels and Zurich.
Choose the US funding route separately.

Brussels is attractive if two of you genuinely relocate and build an EU operating team. Zurich is attractive if the Swiss founder anchors real governance and you want a supervised mission institution. Zug gains weight when a token/protocol project becomes concrete.

At $10m, compare a Belgian public-benefit foundation (FUP) with a Swiss charitable foundation. A Belgian private foundation is not automatically the cheapest answer once reserve taxation is included.

  1. Mostly US foundation grants? Ask about direct international grants before paying an intermediary.
  2. Mostly US individuals / donor-advised funds? Use an eligible US charity route; compare a hosted fund with your own 501(c)(3).
  3. SaaS becomes substantial? Evaluate a taxable subsidiary owned by the mission entity.
  4. Crypto later? Keep the option open. A Swiss subsidiary can be added; a second foundation has independent duties and no shares.
Pay

Good salaries are possible.

Belgium, Switzerland and a US public charity can pay founders for real work at defensible market rates. A $10m grant is funding capacity, not a salary entitlement. [1] [3] [8]

Control

Influence without ownership.

Three seats out of seven plus delegated executive authority fits your stated goal. Mission assets and a foundation-owned subsidiary belong to the organisation, not the founders.

Your location

Work location drives payroll.

A Swiss or US employer does not turn work performed from France into Swiss or US payroll economics. Genuine relocation matters more than the address on the foundation deed. [12]

Three distinct questions: Can the entity legally do it? Will it retain charitable/tax privileges? Will a donor or intermediary accept it? “Foundation”, open-source software and decentralisation do not automatically answer all three.

02 / What changes the answer?

Start with the funding reality.

The same country can be a good or poor choice depending on who funds you and where the team actually works.

US foundation grants → Brussels or Zurich can work directly.

For a few large institutional grants, ask funders whether they support foreign grantees using equivalency determination or expenditure responsibility. This can remove the need for a donation-percentage intermediary. Choose the operating home around the team, governance and tax treatment of reserves.

Decision trigger: get the largest likely funder’s grantee requirements before incorporating.

Short term · 0–12 months

One operating home

  • Confirm charitable purpose for open civic infrastructure and research.
  • Choose Brussels if moving is real; compare Zurich if Swiss stewardship is preferred; price a French association if staying.
  • Set up local payroll, independent board and the first grant agreement.
  • Use a US hosted route only where the donor channel needs it.

At low funding: a suitable fiscal sponsor may be cheaper than forming and running a standalone foundation.

Mid term · 1–3 years

Separate actual activities

  • At recurring US individual fundraising, compare your own US charity with hosted fees.
  • At material SaaS revenue, model a taxable subsidiary and shared-cost/IP agreements.
  • With multi-million reserves, revisit Belgian private foundation vs FUP.
  • Expand salary comparisons and governance as the organisation grows.

Trigger: recurring income and a specific operating need, rather than a round funding number alone.

Long term · 3+ years

Institution + operating arms

  • Mission entity stewards grants, open-source assets and public-benefit programmes.
  • Commercial arm runs enterprise hosting, support and contracts where appropriate.
  • Swiss protocol company or separate foundation only if activities and governance justify it.
  • Review treasury, recurring revenue and executive pay using comparable institutions.

Tradeoff: more entities add boards, accounts, intercompany tax work and operational substance requirements.

03 / Funding & economics

Donation size is only half the story.

A single $10m grant, $10m of small gifts, and $10m paid over five years have different costs and obligations.

Direct international grant

US grantmaking foundation

For a US private foundation, an eligible foreign grantee can be supported through equivalency determination or expenditure responsibility. These require documentation/oversight; they are not automatic.

No mandatory intermediary percentage. One major donor may complicate public-charity equivalency; expenditure responsibility can still be a route for a qualifying charitable project. [6]

US charitable recipient

Individuals, companies & DAFs

Direct gifts to Belgian, French or Swiss entities generally do not give US individuals a federal charitable deduction. A US public charity intermediary can accept eligible gifts, issue receipts and approve overseas grants.

A DAF sponsor has its own foreign-grant policies. Do not assume every DAF will fund a foreign entity directly or accept every intermediary. [5–7]

Own US institution

501(c)(3) main / friends entity

Can be the main charity or an additional US fundraising organisation. It needs genuine governance, charitable discretion and a defensible public-charity classification—not a receipt-issuing conduit.

Costs: formation, $600 full Form 1023 fee, accounting, annual federal/state filings and applicable solicitation registrations, plus overseas arrangements. [17–18]

04 / Founders, pay & decision rights

Comfortable pay. Real oversight.

Your executive job, your board seat and your ownership of assets are separate things.

Initial salary model for discussion

For genuinely senior, full-time roles, a starting budget assumption could be €120k–€170k gross in Brussels and CHF 160k–CHF 220k gross in Switzerland. The earlier €150k / CHF 190k examples are proposals to benchmark, not verified market medians or legal safe harbours.

Location and function matter: CEO, CTO and partnerships lead need not be paid equally. Define annual gross consistently, including contractual holiday/13th-month pay, and add employer contributions, pension, insurance and benefits.

No common payroll uplift: obtain an actual quote for each worker. A Belgian director’s mandate may have self-employed treatment; a separate employee role requires real employment conditions.

Can that become $700k?

Possible in some large organisations; not an automatic growth right. Belgium and Swiss charitable structures do not provide a universal salary ceiling that makes $700k categorically impossible, but the package must remain justified for the work, market and organisation. A US public charity also requires reasonable compensation.

At Agora’s proposed stage, paying each founder $700k is a very different proposition from hiring three senior people comfortably. Review total salary, bonuses, retirement benefits and related-entity pay together.

Commercial income does not make excess founder benefits permissible. A taxable subsidiary still needs defensible related-party compensation and contracts.

The Linux Foundation comparison needs a different frame. The Linux Foundation is a 501(c)(6) business league, with approximately $220.7m revenue in FY2024, predominantly programme-service revenue. Its filing reports James Zemlin at $952,166 reportable compensation + $451,018 other compensation. These are filing categories, not a simple cash base salary. It shows that large nonprofit packages exist; it does not establish a Belgian/Swiss charitable salary benchmark or give US donors a charitable deduction. [19]

Illustrative governance design

7 directors = 3 founders + 4 independents

  • Independent chair and an unconflicted remuneration committee.
  • Founders set product, research and hiring direction within delegated authority and an approved budget.
  • All affected founders leave deliberation and voting on a joint founder-pay decision.
  • Document roles, external compensation comparisons, approval and total benefits.
  • Reserved board decisions: executive appointment/removal, budget, major IP transfers, subsidiary creation and mission changes.

Influence is real; employment is not guaranteed. An independent board must be able to disagree and replace executives.

Your actual location scenarios

Three people can mean three payroll files

  • Stay 2 France + 1 Switzerland: French arrangements for French work, Swiss arrangements for Swiss work, regardless of parent’s address.
  • Move 2 to Brussels: Belgian residence/work and local payroll become relevant; the Swiss founder need not move.
  • Choose US parent: European work obligations remain; no US relocation or work authorisation is assumed.
  • Multi-state work: travel/remote-working patterns can change social-security allocation and need separate assessment.

French foreign-employer registration can avoid forming a French subsidiary solely for payroll, but does not resolve corporate effective-management or permanent-establishment questions. [12]

Founder seat/appointment rights must be drafted for the chosen form. Three of seven is a minority of seats; special vetoes, appointment powers or supermajority thresholds can still produce effective control.

05 / Earned income & protocols

Define the activity before the entity.

Charging for Agora, receiving crypto donations and operating a token-based financial service are three different cases.

Can a Belgian foundation sell SaaS?

Yes, under entity law. It can carry out economic activities and retain surplus for its purpose. It cannot distribute profits to founders. This does not guarantee exemption from corporate tax, VAT or donor-eligibility restrictions. [1, pp. 8, 22–25]

Public-benefit hosting or educational services may be easier to connect to the mission than ordinary enterprise software sold at market rates. “We spend the profits on good causes” is not by itself a complete exemption argument.

If SaaS becomes a major engine: model a wholly foundation-owned taxable company with appropriately priced IP licences/services, cost allocation and clear staffing. Check parent taxation, dividend withholding and VAT; profits do not flow back tax-free by default.

Is Zug better than Brussels for crypto?

Often for ecosystem access; not automatically for the law. Zug has a recognised protocol/crypto network. Zurich has significant technical and financial expertise. Both use Swiss federal financial-market law and FINMA supervision.

Belgium and France operate under MiCA for activities in its scope, with an EU-authorisation/passport framework. A Swiss licence does not provide an EU MiCA passport. At this brief’s date, the maximum MiCA transition ending 1 July 2026 has passed. [15–16]

A Swiss token foundation can be taxable. Building an ecosystem that enriches tokenholders is not automatically the same as advancing a tax-exempt charitable purpose.

A Swiss company: a Belgian foundation can generally be its shareholder, including sole shareholder, subject to its own purpose and duties. Share ownership provides appointment/voting rights; directors still owe the company legal duties. A Swiss GmbH requires CHF 20k paid capital; an AG requires CHF 100k nominal capital, normally at least CHF 50k paid. Both need Swiss-resident representation. [20]

06 / Open the details

The points that can change the choice.

Read only the jurisdictions and issues relevant to your next decision.

Brussels — private foundation, FUP, or association?Operational flexibility vs reserve tax

Private foundation: notarial deed, no members/shareholders, no fixed statutory minimum endowment, purpose-based assets and flexible statutory board arrangements. “Private” is a legal form, not permission to treat assets as founder wealth.

FUP: public-benefit recognition by royal decree adds setup and purpose-change formalities. It is excluded from annual patrimonial tax. That makes it important to price from the outset for a multi-million grant reserve.

ASBL/AISBL: legitimate member-based operating alternatives if you want a membership/consortium model. They do not generally eliminate patrimonial tax. A member-controlled organisation is a different governance design from a memberless foundation.

Tax layers: exemption from corporate income tax, Belgian deductible-gift approval, FUP status and VAT treatment are separate. Legal-entities tax can still tax investment income. Substantial commercial operations or active subsidiary management can require further tax analysis.

Pay/governance: reasonable compensation for actual work; statutory financial-conflict procedures require disclosure, recusal and minutes. Approval by friends who are economically dependent on you is weak evidence of independence.

Belgian donor approval: Philea’s 2024 profile reports specific administration/fundraising limits for approved deductible-gift institutions (20% / 30% respectively). These are not a general ceiling on all programme salaries or all foundations. Have advisers confirm applicability, current rules and programme-cost allocation before seeking that approval.

At $10m: calculate the real 1 January taxable reserve, FUP eligibility and recognition timing. Charity form does not make every grant receipt tax-free under every possible activity profile; review the planned grant and business model together. [1–2]

Zurich — the credible Swiss charitable optionExplicit pay guidance; real supervision

Charity status is not automatic. A foundation is an asset-dedication form. Cantonal/federal exemption requires qualifying public/charitable purposes and irrevocable dedication. Open-source civic infrastructure needs a public-benefit case based on actual beneficiaries and activities.

Compensation: Zurich’s practice note, effective 1 February 2024, explicitly permits reasonable remuneration of foundation-board members. Foundation supervision reviews reasonableness; the tax authority may intervene when it is doubtful. Executive employee pay should be documented separately from board fees. This does not establish any particular salary as acceptable.

Supervision: national/international scope normally means federal ASF supervision, even with a Zurich seat. Annual reporting and an auditor normally apply; exemption from audit is narrow and unsuitable to assume for a $10m case. Compensation totals must be disclosed to the supervisor under its requirements.

Presence: ASF’s published practice requires a Swiss-domiciled board member with individual signature, or a compliant pair of Swiss-domiciled joint signatories including a board member. One Swiss founder can help, but signature arrangements must be designed expressly.

Commercial expansion: subsidiary holdings may be possible, but Swiss charitable exemption has constraints where an entrepreneurial purpose or management role predominates. Zurich’s favourable rules for entrepreneurial grantmaking models are not a blanket exemption for commercial SaaS subsidiaries.

Fit: choose for genuine Swiss anchoring and stewardship, not merely to pay Swiss salaries to people working in France. [3–4]

Zug — protocol ecosystem, same federal rulesA tax-exempt crypto foundation is not the default

Main advantage: a concentrated network of crypto/protocol organisations, advisers and service providers. This is an ecosystem judgment, not proof of easier licensing or banking for a specific project.

Same federal foundation and FINMA framework as Zurich. International activity may still mean federal ASF supervision. Cantonal tax practice and taxable-company rates differ; request a Zug-specific charitable/tax assessment rather than relying on the Zurich practice note.

Token treasury: determine whether sale proceeds are donations, service/prepayment revenue, capital-like funding or another category; classification follows rights and obligations. Valuation and volatile treasury holdings affect runway. A governance token can have payment/investment functions.

What to locate there: an owned Swiss company for a defined operating business, or a foundation for genuinely independent stewardship. Taxable profit should reflect functions, people, assets and risks—not just a registered seat.

Fit: becomes compelling when Swiss protocol operations, funding relationships or a local team are substantive. “We might do crypto someday” is not yet a strong incorporation reason. [4] [13, 16, 20]

France — compare the practical forms, not just a FRUPCheap to start can coexist with stricter leadership tax rules

Association loi 1901: natural lean operating comparator if two founders stay in France. It can employ people and earn revenue. Grant eligibility, donation receipts and commercial-tax status depend on purpose and activities.

Fonds de dotation: a memberless mission/endowment vehicle, with at least €15k initial cash contribution during the first accounting year and at least three directors. It can conduct a public-interest mission or fund another nonprofit. Annual resources above €10k trigger a statutory auditor; public subsidy funding is generally prohibited, with exceptional statutory routes requiring separate review. Public fundraising requires authorisation. Draft whether and how endowment principal can be spent.

FRUP: recognised by decree after a substantial process; official guidance calls for at least €1.5m initial endowment. A $10m grant does not make this the simplest operating form, nor mean all grant money must become permanently locked endowment.

Leadership compensation: preserving “gestion désintéressée” involves rules for legal and de facto leaders. The ¾-SMIC tolerance and the larger-resource regime are not ordinary employee salary bands. The latter has limits on the number of paid leaders, resource history, transparency and remuneration (linked to three social-security ceilings). Exact indexed amounts change.

Your hybrid role matters: a founder who is a genuine employee under independent direction is not identical to a paid controlling leader. Removing a board title does not by itself prevent “dirigeant de fait” treatment. Do not assume three newly created, founder-run paid leadership jobs fit an exemption immediately.

Fit: strongest case is location alignment and a lean association; less attractive for the combination of founder direction plus very high charitable executive pay. SaaS may be taxed/sectorised or placed in a subsidiary. [9–11]

US — public charity, private foundation, or Linux-style trade association?Classification can matter more than the word “foundation”

501(c)(3) public charity: the usual target for deductible public donations. State incorporation is separate from IRS recognition. An open civic-tech project must show a qualifying charitable, educational or scientific purpose; free/open-source software alone does not establish one.

Public support: reliance on one private donor for $10m can affect classification. Public-support calculations, possible unusual-grant treatment, initial years and the donor’s own status matter. One grant does not automatically make you a private foundation, but an independent majority board alone does not make you a public charity.

Private foundation: a valid charitable category, but with a different regime: self-dealing rules, investment-income excise tax, distribution requirements and restrictions on holdings/expenditures. A private operating foundation is another possible category for qualifying direct activities, not a shortcut to assume.

Pay: for a public charity, the IRS rebuttable-presumption procedure uses advance unconflicted approval, appropriate comparable data and contemporaneous documentation. Form 990/Schedule J disclosures make senior compensation visible. Private-foundation self-dealing exceptions need their own analysis.

Earned income: mission-related programme revenue may be compatible with exemption. An unrelated regular business may owe UBIT; using its profits for the mission does not make the activity related. A taxable subsidiary may help separate commercial operations.

501(c)(6): a business league/trade association serving a common industry interest can fit a membership-led ecosystem organisation. It is not a substitute for a 501(c)(3) charitable donation channel. The Linux Foundation parent is this type; member dues/services and charity donations should not be conflated.

Fit: strong for recurring US donor access and a substantive US institution. A purely European team should budget overseas payroll and cross-border reporting rather than treating Delaware incorporation as the complete structure. [5–8, 17–19]

Other issues worth deciding earlyIP, neutrality, treasury, grants, banking and future funding

Protect the public-benefit core

  • IP and licences: establish who owns existing code, trademarks, data rights and new work. Open-source licences already granted may not be withdrawable. Transfers into a foundation may be difficult to reverse.
  • Civic neutrality: distinguish deliberation/research infrastructure from candidate or party support. US 501(c)(3) campaign intervention is prohibited; lobbying is limited. Donor grants can impose additional limits.
  • Restricted grants: clarify allowed salaries, overhead, regranting, endowment consumption, token spending and commercial development.
  • Control over time: appointment terms, vacancies, removal, deadlocks and mission amendments matter as much as the initial 3/7 split.

Make the operating model durable

  • Cash vs promise: a $10m commitment paid in instalments is not $10m available cash. Plan budget and fee timing from actual receipts.
  • FX and treasury: USD grants vs EUR/CHF salaries create exchange risk. Crypto holdings add volatility and valuation complexity.
  • Banking: verify a bank/payment processor accepts the real donor countries, source of funds and token plans before relying on it.
  • Hiring: local labour law, immigration, pension and employer obligations follow real arrangements.
  • Equity finance: a foundation has no founder equity to sell. Outside investment into a subsidiary can dilute its control; charitable assets cannot subsidise founder-owned value without proper terms.
  • Digital regulation: GDPR, applicable Swiss/US privacy law, platform responsibilities and public-sector procurement are separate from foundation law.

  3. The salary ranges were planning proposals. They were not legal allowances or sufficiently verified role/location compensation studies.
  4. Linux Foundation is not a 501(c)(3) salary precedent. Its parent is a much larger 501(c)(6), with different funding economics and filing compensation categories.
  5. Myriad publishes a large-single-donation cap. A straight $67k calculation for one $10m gift misses it; obtain the provider’s written calculation.
  6. A Swiss foundation is not an owned subsidiary. Appointment rights can create influence; its purpose, assets and directors’ duties remain its own.
  7. “Decentralised” does not switch regulation off. Actual token rights, control, services and markets served determine the analysis.
  8. A mixed-country team is feasible, not administratively free. Foreign-employer registration and effective-management/tax-substance questions are distinct.
07 / Practical conclusion

For your next decision, compare two complete structures.

A · Brussels: private foundation vs FUP, with two genuine Brussels-based founders and one Swiss worker; direct US institutional grants and a hosted US route where needed.

B · Zurich: Swiss charitable foundation with real Swiss governance; two France-based or genuinely relocated founders; the same separately selected US funding route.

Keep Zug as a concrete protocol-operation option; keep a French association in the comparison if the two founders stay in France; put an own US public charity on the table if sustained US individual fundraising or a funder requirement justifies it.

The decisive inputs: the first major donor’s requirements, where you will actually live/work, the taxable reserve, the commercial activity forecast, and the specific powers of any future protocol operator.
